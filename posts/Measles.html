Where's The Herd Heading?

A 2025 Look Into the Comeback of Measles, The Disease Thought Eradicated In 2000

Posted by Angel Alberto Aldaco on November 15, 2025

Despite being declared eliminated in the United States in 2000, Measles has seen a large uptick in cases 25 years later, with a major outbreak this year in Gaines County, Texas. Conjointly, vaccination rates, attributed to eradicating the disease in 2000, have sharply dropped across the country. This has come at what medical experts view to be a great loss, as communities across America lose “Herd Immunity”, a threshold of vaccinated rates to ensure that the general population is protected from the disease. In the case of Measles, it requires the community to achieve a 95% vaccination rate.

(Note: This is ultimately an incomplete picture as many counties do not have reportable numbers)

Though herd immunity through strong vaccination rates is what eradicated the disease in 2000, there has been an uptick in MMR vaccine skepticism in recent years. According to a Kaiser Family Foundation Report, in 2025, around 1 in 6 parents (17%) reported delaying or skipping shots, whereas in 2023, it was around 10% of parents. Note, that in order to maintain herd immunity, only 5% of the population, or 1 in 20 people can afford to be unimmunized.

Similarly, in 2023, only a third of adults ruled the claims that MMR vaccines cause autism are “definitely false”, while the remaining population was open to claims that vaccines cause autism. These well refuted claims originally spawned of a now declared fraudulent 1998 scientific paper by Andrew Wakefield, whom Heath and Human Services (HHS) Secartary Robert F Kennedy Jr. has defended. Mr. Wakefield’s paper was retracted after the editor’s investigations found it’s statements to be “utterly false”.

A more thorough way to look at these changes in vaccination rates would be to go county by county. Take, as an example, the larger state of Texas. The lack of Herd Immunity in Gaines County, Texas, is less likely to hurt the community of Webb County, Texas, which is across the state and has obtained Herd Immunity. Upon that analysis, we still have a larger number of counties that sway against vaccinations.

(Note: This is ultimately an incomplete picture as many counties do not have reportable numbers)

Gaines County, Texas, in March of this year, faced a historic outbreak of measles, climbing to 414 cases at the time of reporting. Tragically, the outbreak has lead to the first recorded death of Measles in the past 10 years. According to the latest data, the county did not have herd immunity at an 81.92% vaccination rate, falling short by more than 10%. In a larger analysis of counties which report vaccination data, only 700 out of the more than 3100 counties meet the 95% threshold for herd immunity. This is additionally, not isolated to Gaines County, as atleast 202 counties have a similar or worse vaccination rate.

In responding to the crisis, HHS Secretary RFK Jr wrote an op-ed in Fox News that “Vaccines not only protect individual children from measles, but contribute to community immunity”. Concerning medical experts however, was his promotion of good nutrition and vitamin A as “the best defense aganist infectious disease” rather than vaccinations. While experts such as Dr. Adam Ratner of the infectious disease committee of the American Academy of Pediatrics awknowedge that nutrition and vitamin A aid the prevention of sereve infectious disease, it doesn’t replace that “the way to deal with a measles outbreak, is to vaccinate people against measles”.

Secretary Kennedy went on to write within the same op-ed that “the choice to vaccinate is a personal”, and did not mention anything to dissuade readers on his previously rebuked and repeated claims that there is a link between vaccines and autism. As the choice for vaccination remains to the public to decide, researchers such as Dr. Mathew Kaing of Stanford worry that if immunization rates remain at the same level, measles “may become a endemic within 20 years”. Let alone if they continue their downward trend.
